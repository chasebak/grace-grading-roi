Grading Play Calculator

You have a card. Which grading company and service level is the best play, or should you sell it raw today? Every payment and payout is valued at the day it actually happens.

Grading means mailing a raw (ungraded) card to a company that inspects it, gives it a grade and seals it in a labelled case. The top grade (a 10, “gem mint”) usually sells for far more than the second grade (a 9), and a lower grade often sells for about the raw price, while you paid the fee and waited either way.

What this does NOT claim

  • The turnaround times are estimates, not guarantees. They are each company's own published estimates; a slow batch moves every number on this page.
  • The odds and the sale prices are your inputs. The starting numbers are one example, the same for every company. Real cards from different companies sell for different prices, so enter your own comps per company.
  • Not investment advice. It is arithmetic on the numbers you type.
  • BGS is not verified. No BGS price or turnaround was read; its rows stay empty and out of every chart and ranking until you type them in.
  • When each company charges is a setting. PSA’s default (after grading) comes from the author’s own orders, not from a PSA page; change it if your orders differ.
  • One flat weekly rate stands in for your other use of the money. A real project may not take the same dollars every week, and its return is not certain.
  • Not modelled: taxes, insurance, a lost or damaged card, membership fees and any upcharge a company adds after grading.
